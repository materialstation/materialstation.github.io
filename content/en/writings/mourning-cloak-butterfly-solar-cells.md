I have always believed that butterflies hide something behind their artistic beauty. Whenever I see a butterfly on a warm spring day, I am amazed at the way it floats through the air, showing off its colorful forewings and hindwings. Although both pairs of wings are fragile, they are strong enough to keep the butterfly’s body in the air and make flying easy.

**This mysterious creature must have had something we needed to discover!**

My questions were answered when I came across a study from the *[nanOntario program led by Professor Uwe Erb](https://mse.utoronto.ca/future/nanontario/ "Source: mse.utoronto.ca")* at the University of Toronto. The program was designed for senior high school students, who learned about both bio-inspiration and nanotechnology applications. ***Such a great opportunity for young prospective scientists!***

In the study **[“Bio-designed Anti-reflective Surfaces”](https://mse.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/nanOntario-Newsletter-Year2.pdf "Source: mse.utoronto.ca")**, students examined the eye structure of ***the Monarch and the [Mourning Cloak](https://www.britannica.com/animal/brush-footed-butterfly "Source: britannica.com") butterflies***, two well-known North American species.

The main difference between these native butterflies comes from the presence of *[the moth-eye effect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1467803915000705 "Source: sciencedirect.com")*. The eyes of the butterflies were examined with a scanning electron microscope to obtain high-resolution images of the eye surface.

First of all, I should remind you that the structure of an insect eye is quite different from that of the human eye. The insect eye is called a compound eye because it consists of repeating units called ommatidia. These units, repeated thousands of times, are closely packed together and form the semi-spherical, faceted structure of an insect’s compound eye.

While the eye surface of the Mourning Cloak butterfly shows the moth-eye effect, the Monarch’s does not. The high-resolution images revealed that the Mourning Cloak’s eye surface is covered with nano-nipples. Thanks to this special surface, a gradual change in refractive index occurs, which reduces the amount of light reflected by the eye. This also explains why only the Mourning Cloak’s eye shows the moth-eye effect: it is due to its anti-reflective surface structure.

**Who could have imagined that the nano-nipple structure of a beautiful butterfly’s eye would lead to a special type of solar cell design!**

**[A study](https://phys.org/news/2008-02-moth-eyes-key-efficient-solar.html "Source: phys.org")** on solar cells reports that solar cells with a moth-eye nipple structure can absorb light and use it efficiently instead of reflecting it.
