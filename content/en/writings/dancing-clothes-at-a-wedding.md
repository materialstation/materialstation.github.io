I remember those times when I was 6 or 7 years old: I would wait for that specific moment, keeping my eyes on the stage just to see people’s faces disappear while their clothes started shining under the dim light. Was it because everyone was dancing in a happy mood? If so, why weren’t everyone’s clothes on the stage shining so vividly? Had I fainted for a few seconds and seen everything in matrix form, in a holographic universe? How come? I also noticed that sometimes even my clothes were shining in a bluish or greenish color, and I would run around curiously with my friends in this bath of color, disturbing the people dancing on the stage.

I was not able to understand the reason for this color game until I started taking science courses in high school. Honestly, when I understood what causes this phenomenon, I was both disappointed and satisfied with the reality. Of course, the 6-year-old little girl was expecting a more extraordinary explanation based on her imagination, but still, reality could not make her forget how much she had enjoyed those moments of not knowing.

If you wonder why I suddenly remembered the old times and the dancing clothes, it is because I was reading Mark Miodownik’s book *Liquid Rules: The Delightful and Dangerous Substances That Flow Through Our Lives*. I read every part of his book with admiration. He explains how and why lovely materials behave in ways that make us think they are magical.

The secret of my dancing clothes was nothing more than fluorescent molecules in the washing powder people used to wash their clothes. In the book, Mr. Miodownik explains that these molecules, also called optical brighteners, bind to the fibers of white clothes and stubbornly stay there after washing. When UV light hit the dance stage, it activated the optical brighteners in people’s white clothes and made me think they were glowing, as if butterflies were swinging on the dance floor!

Such a lovely memory, being surrounded by the magic of materials! Isn’t it?
