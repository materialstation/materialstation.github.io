Gold is one of the shining elements of the periodic table. This beautiful material has a unique combination of chemical and physical properties across different scales of matter, from macro to nano. As a metallic material with excellent corrosion resistance, high density (19.32 g/cm³) and malleability, it is one of the most preferred materials in a wide range of applications. It should come as no surprise that gold-covered artifacts remain unchanged after centuries underground. Unlike its metal counterparts such as silver and copper, gold is quite reluctant to react with atmospheric components.

Gold has therefore held a **[reputable place](https://www.routledge.com/Biogeochemical-Health-and-Ecotoxicological-Perspectives-on-Gold-and-Gold/Eisler/p/book/9780367393694 "Source: routledge.com")** throughout history, not only in jewelry and coinage but also in electronics, medicine, physiology and immunology.

The ancient Egyptians are known to have used gold for jewelry and to decorate their tombs, crypts, statues and sarcophagi.

Let’s keep the physical properties of gold in mind and try to figure out the connection between gold and cultural rituals.

**What could be the symbolic reason?**

Gold was believed to represent the **[flesh of the gods](https://www.thoughtco.com/colors-of-ancient-egypt-43718 "Source: thoughtco.com")**, so the death masks of royals were made of gold. Gold was also a symbol of eternal life and indestructibility. The revered royals had to be protected from **[evil spirits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_mask "Source: en.wikipedia.org")** on their journey into the afterlife, and gold was used to strengthen the spirit of the mummy for this purpose.

The same spiritual connection with gold was also made in the age of the Greek heroes.

The death mask of the so-called heroic King Agamemnon was made of gold, too. The masks probably emphasized the importance of the deceased, since gold masks were not placed on every body buried in the same burial ground. Social status differences among the deceased were also reflected in the **[conspicuous consumption of gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_masks_of_Mycenae "Source: en.wikipedia.org")** in the death masks.
