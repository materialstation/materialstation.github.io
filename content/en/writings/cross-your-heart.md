Your efforts are not always rewarded, and sometimes you may need to go back to where you started. Especially for a scientist, things do not always go smoothly or as planned during an experiment. However, challenges can become an opportunity instead of a source of disappointment. I was aware of the power of **[self-sabotage](https://ecorner.stanford.edu/videos/the-enlightened-entrepreneur-entire-talk/ "Source: ecorner.stanford.edu")** over my decisions, so I decided to change my point of view and tried to see the moment of crisis as an opportunity. If you work with materials that are invisible to the naked eye, you have to learn to be patient. What you want to see may not match what you see through the microscope.

That day I was very nervous when I entered the lab. The thought of not finding a suitable device was heartbreaking, because this was a long process that started with patterning the substrate by optical lithography. The substrate was etched with chemicals before the gold deposition. Why gold? Because gold is a great conductor. The next step was drilling holes, that is, opening gaps, with a Focused Ion Beam (FIB). Opening the gap was critical, because we needed a gap to suspend the nanowires between the gold contacts.

After completing the **[fabrication process of the device](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/16373 "Source: intechopen.com")**, it was time to take a close look at it with SEM. I was very worried, because I had to prepare at least one good-looking sample for the measurement. During imaging, I desperately zoomed in and out and carefully checked the cross-section view of the sample, but the results were not good. I would have cried if I had not noticed the secret message given to me through a damaged electrode. It was such a miraculous moment! I should not have been so weak and desperate! The crossed nanowires on the heart-shaped broken electrode piece yelled at me: *“Never stop believing in yourself! Cross your heart!”*
