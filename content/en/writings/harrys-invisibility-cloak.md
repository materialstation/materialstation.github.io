However, we are smart enough to figure out the marvelous feature of *[chameleon skin](https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/colour-changing-artificial-chameleon-skin-powered-by-nanomachines "Source: cam.ac.uk")* and copy it to build an ***invisibility cloak!***

*You don’t need to be jealous of Harry’s invisibility cloak anymore!*

Researchers at *[Cambridge](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/adom.201900951 "Source: wiley.com")* have already developed an artificial ‘chameleon skin’ that could be used for camouflage.

The color-changing skill of chameleons stems from the presence of *chromatophores*, which are skin cells. These cells have connected contractile fibers that make it possible to move pigments around. When the skin is exposed to light, the pigments either spread out to display their color or squeeze together to make the cell clear.

The artificial chromatophores developed in Cambridge work on the same principle, but instead of contractile fibers, their color-changing ability depends on a light-powered nanomechanism, and tiny droplets of water act as the cells. These microdroplets contain gold nanoparticles coated in a polymer shell, and how closely the nanoparticles pack together determines which color appears. The color change of this fantastic material can be seen when it is heated above 32 °C.
