The synthesis of materials plays a very important role in the production and development of advanced materials, especially in nanoscience and nanotechnology applications. The outstanding properties of nanomaterials can be utilized only when they are produced with the desired *[size, shape, crystal structure, morphology and chemical composition](https://ccsuniversity.ac.in/bridge-library/pdf/L-3%20Synthesis%20of%20Nanostructured%20Materials%20Prof%20BPS.pdf "Source: ccsuniversity.ac.in")*. Therefore, understanding materials synthesis approaches is crucial for different applications in nanotechnology.

*[The synthesis of nanomaterials](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2021/ma/d0ma00807a "Source: pubs.rsc.org")* is divided into two approaches: top-down and bottom-up methods.

In the top-down method, bulk materials are used to produce new nanostructured materials. In the bottom-up method, on the other hand, nanomaterials are built up atom by atom or molecule by molecule.

The method I used in the lab was ***[mechanical exfoliation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23132225/ "Source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov")***, one of the top-down methods. Mechanical exfoliation can overcome the weak attraction forces between the layers of 2D materials and produce atomically thin layers. It is basically performed by applying adhesive tape to the bulk material and pulling it away very quickly, peeling off layers of the material to leave *[atomic layer flakes](https://www.azonano.com/article.aspx?ArticleID=5466 "Source: azonano.com")* on a substrate.

The possibility of fabricating atomic-scale devices makes 2D materials important for the electronics industry. As the surface area of 2D materials increases, their physical and chemical reactivity also increases, and as they become thinner, quantum confinement effects appear. Therefore, the ultrathin forms of these materials show outstanding photonic, electronic and magnetic properties compared with their bulk counterparts.

Graphene is known as the first 2D material; it is a semimetal with a zero bandgap and extraordinary conductivity.

Before starting material synthesis with mechanical exfoliation, I mentally prepared myself for any situation, since this method is notorious for its poor yield. So I kept performing mechanical exfoliation on a silicon substrate, and each time I checked what I had obtained under the optical microscope.

Most of them seemed to be quite thick crystals, but suddenly I noticed something magnificent!

**Oh! Was it a nano leaf that I saw among the crystal pieces?**

Yes. It must have been a leaf, because before coming to the lab I had gone out for a walk on a rainy day, so I had probably brought that leaf into the lab with me.

Or my brain was playing tricks on me! It was nothing more than an exfoliated crystal that looked like a leaf.

Well… I’ve made up my mind. It was a very beautiful autumn leaf!

**Nano leaf!**
