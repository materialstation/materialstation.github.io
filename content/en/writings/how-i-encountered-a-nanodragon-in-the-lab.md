It was just a regular day at the laboratory. First, I mechanically exfoliated a WS₂ (tungsten disulfide) crystal onto a silicon dioxide substrate. My goal was to find the thinnest possible crystal to make devices for testing optoelectronic applications. After checking the messy crystals lying on the square substrate under an optical microscope, I decided to switch to a scanning electron microscope (SEM), which was the best option for studying the surface morphology of a material. Unfortunately, the colors disappeared in the SEM, because electrons are used as the imaging source.

As a result, I was left alone with strangely shaped crystals on a grayish background.

Very carefully and patiently, I was rolling the mouse up and down in the hope of finding something that would work for me. Each movement on the sample surface showed up on the screen with a 5-second delay. I had to endure this slow movement, yet I also had to be quick, because I had limited time to take images before the next user’s reservation started. As time went on, I found a few crystals that could be used in the device fabrication process. I took out my lab notebook to write down the positions of the crystals on the substrate, because the SEM shows the sample surface at a much higher magnification than an optical microscope. I needed to make my life easier by taking notes, which would save me time in the next step.

I was content with the outcome, but something in my head kept telling me to keep looking for the best. This ambition and perfectionism could make me either a hero or a fool by the end of the day. Nevertheless, I listened to that voice once again.

In the last 10 minutes, I did faster scans of each corner of the substrate. In one of these scans, I encountered the nanodragon. I was so excited when I looked at the weird shape of the crystal. The funny thing is that I devoted myself to making people believe what I saw in my head. In the following days, I installed Photoshop and started to play with the SEM image of the WS₂ crystal. At the end of the day, I was proudly showing the result to my friends.

Even though they laughed at my cute nanodragon, I didn’t give up dreaming, because I sincerely believe Einstein’s words: *“Logic will lead you from A to B. Imagination will take you everywhere.”*
